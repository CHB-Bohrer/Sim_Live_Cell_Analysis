# The scientific question: does distance set transcription?

::: {.box}
**Short version.** The final readout is a curve: the probability that the promoter is bursting, as a function of enhancer-promoter distance. Measured distances are blurred and, in 2D, shortened. A fit that models the measurement recovers the curve that was put in; a fit that ignores it does not. With realistic polymer motion, the distance a moment ago matters more than the distance now.
:::

## The fit

The curve is

$$P(\text{ON} \mid d) = p_\text{far}\left(1 + \frac{F - 1}{1 + (d / d_{1/2})^4}\right),$$

with $p_\text{far}$ the probability far from the enhancer, $F$ the fold change at contact and $d_{1/2}$ the half-effect distance. The true distance $d$ is never seen. What is seen is a measured distance $m$, related to $d$ by a kernel $K(m \mid d)$ that contains localization error, any colour shift and, for 2D imaging, projection. The fit integrates over the unknown $d$, using a prior for the population of true distances estimated from the data, and gets its intervals by resampling cells.

Because $F$ alone is not identified when there is no effect, the reported effect size is the **contrast**, $P(\text{ON at 150 nm}) / P(\text{ON at 1000 nm})$.

## Does it recover a known curve?

A curve with $F = 8$ and $d_{1/2} = 350$ nm was put in, observed through 2D projection with 70 nm localization error, and fitted (Figure 4).

![Recovery of a known curve. The corrected fit (blue) lies on the curve put in (dashed); the fit that ignores measurement error (orange) is too flat. Generated by `checks.py ep_recovery`.](figures/val-ep-recovery.svg)

## What the scans found

Recorded results from the parameter scans, all with placeholder error sizes:

- **2D projection** costs about 35% of the effect if ignored, and is fully corrected by the fit.
- **Wrong spot picks are the damaging error.** 5%, 10% and 20% wrong picks lower the contrast by 6%, 13% and 24%. The fit corrects this if the share of wrong picks is supplied; it cannot be estimated from the distances themselves.
- Localization precision matters only above about 200 nm. Missed frames are harmless up to 60%. A colour shift up to 100 nm is harmless.
- With no real effect, the method reports one in 3 to 7% of simulated populations, close to the nominal 5%.

## The finding that changed the analysis

With distances from the polymer simulations, the enhancer-promoter distance loses its memory in about 9 s, while the promoter integrates over about 45 s. So $P(\text{ON})$ against the distance **now** is nearly flat (contrast 1.36). The curve that can be recovered is $P(\text{ON})$ against the distance **history** (reference contrast 4.19, time constant 45 s recovered).

For the three imaging modes at 600 cells for one hour, the recorded contrasts were 4.78 (2D), 4.34 (z-stack) and 4.39 (biplane), each with a scatter of about 1. The largest single error is taking the two colours 0.15 s apart (about 120 nm per axis). Frames must be no more than 10 s apart.

These numbers inherit the assumed polymer time scale (Section 3) **[Assumption]**.

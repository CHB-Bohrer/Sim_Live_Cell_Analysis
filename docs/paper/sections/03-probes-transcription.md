# Probes and transcription: how bright, and when

::: {.box}
**Short version.** Two small stochastic models decide what each locus looks like. Probes bind and leave, which sets how bright a locus is in each frame. A promoter switches on and off, polymerases start while it is on, and each polymerase carries MS2 loops. The enhancer distance changes one chosen rate. Both models are simulated exactly and both have exact theory to check against.
:::

## Probe binding

Each locus has $N$ probes. A probe cycles through three states:

$$\text{free} \xrightarrow{k_\text{bind}} \text{bound} \xrightarrow{k_\text{scan}} \text{scanning} \xrightarrow{k_\text{off}} \text{free}, \qquad \text{bound} \xrightarrow{k_\text{unbind}} \text{free}.$$

A locus can also have a limited number of binding sites (`capacity`). The simulation is an exact Gillespie algorithm. Without a capacity limit the probes are independent, and balancing the flows gives the stationary ratios

$$\frac{B}{F} = \frac{k_\text{bind}}{k_\text{scan} + k_\text{unbind}}, \qquad \frac{S}{B} = \frac{k_\text{scan}}{k_\text{off}},$$

where $F$, $B$ and $S$ are the mean numbers free, bound and scanning. The brightness of a locus in a frame is photons per probe per second, times the exposure, times the number of probes attached ($B + S$) averaged over the exposure.

All rates and the photon yield are placeholders **[Assumption]**. Not modelled: bleaching of single probes, probe positions along the locus, a probe pool shared between loci.

## Transcription and MS2

The promoter is a two-state switch, $\text{OFF} \xrightarrow{k_\text{on}} \text{ON} \xrightarrow{k_\text{off}} \text{OFF}$. While it is ON, polymerases start at rate $k_\text{init}$. Each polymerase gains MS2 loops as it crosses the cassette, carries all 24 until the end of the gene, waits a random dwell time and leaves. The MS2 signal is the number of loops carried by all polymerases on the gene.

The enhancer acts through the promoter-enhancer distance $d(t)$, which multiplies **one** chosen rate ($k_\text{on}$ by default) by

$$1 + (F - 1)\,f(d), \qquad f(d) = \frac{1}{1 + (d / d_{1/2})^{n}} \quad\text{(default: } d_{1/2} = 400\ \text{nm},\ n = 4).$$

$F$ is the fold change at contact. Other shapes are available (a sharp contact threshold, an exponential, or none as a control). All rates are per second of real time, so the frame interval can be changed freely.

The default gene is deliberately bursty: OFF for 20 minutes on average, ON for 45 seconds, a start every 6.7 s while ON, so about 7 polymerases per burst and about 3 bursts per hour **[Assumption]**. With 10 s frames a burst covers only 4 or 5 frames, and the code warns about it.

Cells can carry more than one copy of the locus pair (`copies.py`). Each copy has its own polymer simulation, probes and transcription, and copy 0 uses exactly the random numbers it would have used alone.

## Does it work?

**Probes.** Six release rates, 60,000 simulated seconds each, compared with the exact stationary theory (Figure 2).

![Probe model: simulation (points) on the exact theory (dashed). Left: mean number attached. Right: its coefficient of variation. Generated by `checks.py probes`.](figures/val-probes.svg)

**Transcription.** A distance that alternates between contact (80 nm) and apart (400 nm) every 200 s, with a sharp-contact coupling of 15-fold on $k_\text{on}$. The mean over 300 simulated cells is compared with the exact time-dependent expectation (Figure 3). The promoter state follows the contact periods with a delay, and the MS2 signal follows the promoter with a further delay. That lag between distance and signal matters later (Section 9).

![Transcription model: mean of 300 cells (blue) on the exact expectation (dashed). Generated by `checks.py transcription`.](figures/val-transcription.svg)

Both checks pass their limits; the numbers are in Section 11.
